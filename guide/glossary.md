## Nutrients and Chemistry

**B — Boron**
A micronutrient required in tiny quantities. Deficiency shows as distorted, thick, or brittle young leaves and poor fruit set.

**Ca — Calcium**
A secondary macronutrient essential for cell wall strength. Deficiency causes tip burn in lettuce and blossom end rot in tomatoes. Hard tap water is often calcium-rich.

**Cu — Copper**
A micronutrient involved in enzyme activity. Deficiency causes wilting and blue-green discolouration of young leaves.

**DTPA — Diethylenetriaminepentaacetic acid**
A type of iron chelate (a molecule that keeps iron in a soluble, plant-available form). DTPA-chelated iron is effective at pH 5.0–7.0 and may give the nutrient solution a brown tint — this is normal.

**EDDHA — Ethylenediamine-di(o-hydroxyphenylacetic acid)**
A stronger iron chelate than DTPA, effective across a wider pH range (4.0–9.0). Used when pH runs high and iron deficiency is persistent.

**EDTA — Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid**
A chelating agent that binds metal ions and keeps them dissolved. Ca-EDTA supplements are sometimes used to improve calcium uptake, particularly in media-based systems.

**EC — Electrical Conductivity**
A measure of how well a solution conducts electricity, used as a proxy for the total concentration of dissolved nutrients. Higher EC = more nutrients dissolved. Measured in mS/cm (millisiemens per centimetre). Target ranges vary by crop and growth stage — see [Guide 02 — Nutrients](nft/02-nutrient-solution.md).

**Fe — Iron**
A micronutrient required for chlorophyll production. Deficiency shows as yellowing between leaf veins (interveinal chlorosis) on young leaves — one of the most common deficiencies in hydroponic systems, especially at high pH.

**GH — General Hydroponics**
A widely used brand of hydroponic nutrients. Their Flora Series (3-part liquid) and MaxiBloom (dry) are commonly referenced in these guides as alternative nutrient options to Masterblend.

**K — Potassium**
A primary macronutrient (the "K" in NPK). Essential for water regulation, enzyme activation, and fruit quality. Deficiency shows as brown leaf edges (scorching), especially in fruiting crops.

**Media EC vs Reservoir EC**
Two distinct EC readings in media-based systems like Ebb & Flow. *Reservoir EC* is what your meter reads in the reservoir; *media EC* is the effective concentration in the root zone, which drifts higher as salts accumulate in the LECA between floods. Compare drain-water EC against reservoir EC to detect accumulation — see Guide 02 (E&F) and Compare 01.

**Mg — Magnesium**
A secondary macronutrient and the central atom in chlorophyll. Deficiency shows as yellowing between the veins (interveinal chlorosis) on older leaves first, often confused with iron deficiency.

**Mn — Manganese**
A micronutrient involved in photosynthesis and enzyme function. Deficiency shows as interveinal chlorosis with brown spots, similar to iron deficiency but starting on younger leaves.

**Mo — Molybdenum**
A micronutrient essential for nitrogen metabolism and enzyme function. Deficiency is rare but can mimic nitrogen deficiency.

**N — Nitrogen**
The first and most abundant primary macronutrient in the NPK ratio. Drives vegetative growth. Deficiency shows as uniform yellowing starting from older (lower) leaves.

**NPK**
The three primary macronutrients — Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), and Potassium (K) — and the ratio in which a fertiliser contains them. A 3:1:2 NPK ratio means 3 parts N to 1 part P to 2 parts K. Ratios shift across growth stages: higher N in vegetative, higher P and K at flowering/fruiting.

**P — Phosphorus**
A primary macronutrient essential for root development, energy transfer (ATP), and flowering. Deficiency shows as purple or red tints on the underside of leaves.

**pH — Potential of Hydrogen**
A scale from 0 to 14 measuring the acidity or alkalinity of a solution. 7.0 is neutral; below 7 is acidic; above 7 is alkaline. The target range for most hydroponic crops is 5.5–6.5. pH directly controls which nutrients are chemically available to plants — nutrients "lock out" at incorrect pH even when present in solution.

**ppm — Parts Per Million**
A unit of concentration. Some EC meters display TDS as ppm rather than mS/cm. A rough conversion: 1.0 mS/cm ≈ 500–700 ppm (varies by conversion factor used by the meter manufacturer).

**S — Sulfur**
A secondary macronutrient involved in amino acid synthesis and enzyme function. Rarely deficient when using standard hydroponic nutrient formulations.

**TDS — Total Dissolved Solids**
The total weight of all dissolved substances in water, measured in ppm (parts per million). Closely related to EC — TDS meters often measure EC and convert to ppm. See also: EC, ppm.

**VPD — Vapour Pressure Deficit**
A measure of the difference between the moisture the air can hold and the moisture it actually holds, at a given temperature. High VPD (dry, hot air) causes plants to transpire rapidly and can stress crops. Relevant to outdoor system management in summer.

**Zn — Zinc**
A micronutrient involved in growth hormone production and enzyme function. Deficiency causes small leaves, short internodes, and distorted growth.

## Measurement Units

**kWh — Kilowatt-hour**
A unit of electrical energy. One kilowatt-hour is the energy consumed by a 1,000 W device running for one hour. Used in these guides for costing supplemental lighting and pump running costs.

**gph — Gallons per Hour (US)**
Flow rate used first in this guide. The NFT greens pump is 160–210 US gph (600–800 L/h). The NFT fruiting pump is 50–100 US gph (200–400 L/h). The Ebb and Flow pump is 250 US gph (950 L/h). A US gallon is 3.785 L, not the 4.55 L imperial gallon.

**L/h — Litres per Hour**
Metric flow rate, shown in brackets after US gph.

**L/min — Litres per Minute**
Channel-level flow. Target NFT flow is 0.26–0.53 US gpm (1–2 L/min) per channel.

**mol/m²/day — Moles per Square Metre per Day**
The unit for DLI (Daily Light Integral). One mole equals approximately 6 × 10²³ photons. A DLI of 12–17 mol/m²/day is typical for leafy crops.

**mS/cm — Millisiemens per Centimetre**
The standard unit for measuring EC (electrical conductivity) in hydroponic solutions. A typical leafy green target is 1.0–2.0 mS/cm; fruiting crops often target 2.0–3.5 mS/cm.

**μmol/m²/s — Micromoles per Square Metre per Second**
The unit for PPFD (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density) — the number of photons hitting a surface per second. A sunny midsummer day delivers around 1,800–2,000 μmol/m²/s at noon.

**RH — Relative Humidity**
The percentage of moisture in the air relative to the maximum it could hold at that temperature. Expressed as a percentage (e.g. 60% RH). Target range for most crops: 50–70%.

## Systems and Methods

**DWC — Deep Water Culture**
A hydroponic method where plant roots are suspended in a constantly aerated, nutrient-rich reservoir. Referenced in comparison tables as a contrast to NFT and Ebb & Flow.

**E&F — Ebb & Flow (Flood-and-Drain)**
A hydroponic method where a grow table or tray is periodically flooded with nutrient solution from a reservoir below, then drained back by gravity. Plants sit in an inert growing medium (typically LECA). The flood-drain cycle is controlled by a timer. This is one of the two primary systems covered in this guide.

**Flood Cycle**
One complete flood-and-drain event: the pump runs 15–30 minutes, solution stops about 3/4 in (2 cm) below the LECA surface, the pump stops, and the table drains back by gravity. Vegetative crops are flooded 3 times a day. Fruiting crops 4 times a day. Four is the ceiling.

**IPM — Integrated Pest Management**
A systematic approach to pest and disease control that combines prevention, monitoring, and targeted intervention, using the least disruptive methods first (physical barriers → biological controls → organic treatments → chemical pesticides as a last resort).

**NFT — Nutrient Film Technique**
A hydroponic method where a continuous thin film of nutrient solution flows along the bottom of slightly sloped channels, bathing the roots of plants held in net pots. Plants are supported without solid growing media in the root zone. This is one of the two primary systems covered in this guide.

**RDWC — Recirculating Deep Water Culture**
A variant of DWC where the nutrient solution is circulated between multiple buckets or containers. Mentioned in these guides in the context of organic hydroponic compatibility.

**RO — Reverse Osmosis**
A water purification process that forces water through a semi-permeable membrane to remove dissolved solids, minerals, and contaminants. Produces very pure, near-zero-EC water. Useful when tap water has high EC (>0.4 mS/cm) or problematic mineral content.

## Growing Media and Materials

**BOM — Bill of Materials**
A complete parts list for a build, typically including component descriptions, quantities, unit costs, and sourcing notes. See [Guide 12 — Budget & Sourcing](nft/12-budget-and-sourcing.md).

**Coco / Coco Coir**
Coconut fibre, a natural growing medium made from processed coconut husks. Used in Zone B microgreens trays and Zone C grow bags in this system. Holds moisture well and has a neutral pH when buffered. Not suitable as primary media in Ebb & Flow flood tables due to excessive water retention.

**EPDM — Ethylene Propylene Diene Monomer**
A synthetic rubber used for sealing washers and gaskets. EPDM washers on bulkhead fittings provide a waterproof seal that resists UV degradation and is safe for contact with nutrient solutions.

**FRP — Fibre Reinforced Plastic**
A composite material (fibreglass) used for commercial-grade flood tables. Stronger and more durable than HDPE but more expensive.

**HDPE — High-Density Polyethylene**
A food-safe thermoplastic widely used for reservoirs, buckets, and flood table liners in hydroponics. Chemically inert, UV-stabilised grades available. Recommended reservoir material in this guide.

**LECA — Lightweight Expanded Clay Aggregate**
Also called clay pebbles. A growing medium made from clay balls fired at high temperature, creating a porous, stable structure. The primary media for Ebb & Flow flood tables in this guide. Key properties: excellent drainage, reusable, pH-neutral, does not compact or decompose.

**PTFE — Polytetrafluoroethylene**
Commonly known by the brand name Teflon. Used as thread-seal tape (PTFE tape) when assembling threaded plumbing fittings. Prevents leaks at all threaded connections on pumps, manifolds, and bulkhead fittings.

**PVC — Polyvinyl Chloride**
A rigid plastic used for NFT channels, pipes, manifolds, and fittings. This build uses 3 in (76 mm) square tube for CH1–CH3 and 4 in (102 mm) square tube for CH4, each 8 ft (2.44 m) long. The greens manifold is 1 in (25 mm). Channel inlets are 1/2 in (13 mm).

## Lighting

**DLI — Daily Light Integral**
The total amount of photosynthetically active light delivered to a surface over an entire day. Calculated as PPFD × hours of light × a conversion factor. Units: mol/m²/day. Leafy greens need 12–17 mol/m²/day; fruiting crops need 20–30+ mol/m²/day.

**LED — Light-Emitting Diode**
A semiconductor light source. Full-spectrum LED grow lights are the most energy-efficient option for supplemental lighting and season extension. Also used in digital meters (e.g. Bluelab Truncheon uses LED indicators).

**PAR — Photosynthetically Active Radiation**
The portion of the light spectrum (wavelengths 400–700 nm) that plants use for photosynthesis. PAR is what grow light specifications measure — not total light output (lumens), which is a human-eye metric.

**PPFD — Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density**
The number of PAR photons hitting one square metre of surface per second. The standard measure of grow light intensity at canopy level. Units: μmol/m²/s. A sunny midsummer noon delivers ~1,800–2,000 μmol/m²/s outdoors.

**UV — Ultraviolet**
Light with wavelengths below 400 nm, outside the visible spectrum. In these guides: (1) UV exposure degrades non-UV-stabilised plastics over time — use UV-stabilised materials outdoors; (2) UV sterilisation can be used to kill algae and pathogens in reservoir water.

## Electronics and Automation

**ADC — Analogue-to-Digital Converter**
A circuit that converts a continuous analogue voltage signal (such as from a pH probe or EC probe) into a digital number that a microcontroller can read. The ESP32 has built-in ADC inputs.

**API — Application Programming Interface**
A defined way for software systems to communicate with each other. In these guides: the Google Sheets API can receive data pushed from an ESP32 over WiFi, enabling cloud-based data logging without a dedicated server.

**Drain Confirmation**
An Ebb & Flow safety check that verifies each flood table has fully drained after a flood cycle, typically via a float switch mounted low on the table wall. If a table is still holding water 45 minutes after pump-off, the drain is blocked — an alert fires and the next flood cycle is paused. The #1 E&F-specific sensor; see Guide 13 (E&F) and Compare 03.

**ESP32 / ESP8266**
Low-cost microcontroller boards with built-in WiFi (and Bluetooth on the ESP32) made by Espressif. The primary recommended automation platform in this guide. The ESP32 is the more capable successor with more GPIO pins, faster processor, and built-in Bluetooth.

**EZO — Atlas Scientific EZO circuit boards**
A range of pre-calibrated, high-accuracy sensor circuits (EZO-EC, EZO-pH, EZO-DO) from Atlas Scientific. More accurate than DIY analogue probes but significantly more expensive ($50–75 per circuit).

**GPIO — General Purpose Input/Output**
The programmable digital pins on a microcontroller. Each GPIO pin can be configured as an input (to read sensors, buttons, float switches) or an output (to control relays, LEDs, pumps). The ESP32 has up to 34 usable GPIO pins.

**GND — Ground**
The common negative reference voltage in an electrical circuit (0 V). All sensors, power supplies, and microcontroller boards in the system must share a common GND for signals to be read correctly.

**HTTP — HyperText Transfer Protocol**
The standard protocol for transferring data over the web. In these guides: ESP32 devices can send HTTP POST requests to push sensor data to cloud services or home automation platforms.

**I2C — Inter-Integrated Circuit**
A two-wire digital communication protocol (SDA + SCL lines) used to connect sensors and displays to a microcontroller. Many sensors in these guides use I2C: BH1750 (light), SHT31 (temperature/humidity), and some EC/pH boards.

**MQTT — Message Queuing Telemetry Transport**
A lightweight publish/subscribe messaging protocol designed for IoT devices. Widely used with home automation platforms (Home Assistant, Node-RED). An ESP32 can publish sensor readings to an MQTT broker, which other devices or dashboards then subscribe to.

**OTA — Over-The-Air**
A method of updating firmware on a microcontroller wirelessly, without physically connecting a USB cable. Supported by ESPHome and Tasmota firmware. Useful once the ESP32 is installed inside a weatherproof enclosure.

**PSU — Power Supply Unit**
A device that converts mains AC voltage to the DC voltage required by electronic components. In these guides: 12 V PSUs power relay modules; 5 V is typically supplied by a USB adapter or the ESP32's onboard regulator.

**SSR — Solid-State Relay**
An electronic switch with no moving parts (unlike a mechanical relay). Used to switch mains voltage loads (pumps, lights, heaters) using a low-voltage signal from a microcontroller. Better for frequent switching; more expensive than mechanical relays.

**USB — Universal Serial Bus**
The standard connector used for programming and powering microcontrollers. The ESP32 is typically programmed via a Micro-USB or USB-C port and can be powered from a standard phone charger.

**VCC**
The positive power supply terminal in an electronic circuit. Components are wired between VCC (positive supply, e.g. 3.3 V or 5 V) and GND (0 V). Labelled on sensor modules and relay boards to indicate which pin receives power.

**WiFi — Wireless Fidelity**
The standard for wireless local networking (IEEE 802.11). The ESP32's built-in WiFi allows it to send alerts, push data to cloud services, and be remotely configured without a physical connection.

## Electrical Safety

**GFCI — Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter**
A safety device that cuts power in milliseconds if current leaks to ground (through water or a person). This guide’s worked example is a 120 V outdoor receptacle with GFCI. In South Africa use a 230 V circuit with a 30 mA earth-leakage breaker.

**RCD — Residual Current Device**
The earth-leakage breaker used in South Africa and much of the world. A 30 mA device is the bracketed equivalent of the US GFCI in this guide.

## General

**DIY — Do It Yourself**
Building or assembling something without professional help. Prices in this guide are US dollars with South African rand in brackets, at a planning rate of $1 = R18 (3 October 2026). Hardware totals live in each system’s Guide 12.

**CSV — Comma-Separated Values**
A simple text file format for storing tabular data (rows and columns separated by commas). Used in automation logging — sensor readings can be saved or exported as a CSV file for analysis in a spreadsheet.

**Pythium**
A water-mould that causes root rot. Infected roots turn brown, slimy, and smell sour. Risk rises when solution stays above 77°F (25°C), oxygen is low, or an Ebb and Flow table never drains. Keep solution at 64–72°F (18–22°C), run NFT pumps 24 hours a day, and fit a drain-confirmation cutoff on Ebb and Flow. See Guide 07 in both tracks.

**ROI — Return on Investment**
A measure of the financial return relative to the cost of a project. In the context of these guides: how long it takes for the value of harvested produce to equal the cost of building and running the system.

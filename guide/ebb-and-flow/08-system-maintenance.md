## 1. Why Maintenance Discipline Matters

An Ebb & Flow system has a fundamentally different maintenance profile from a continuous-flow system like NFT. Every flood cycle is an intervention: the table fills, roots are bathed, and the solution drains away. If any part of that cycle misfires — the pump sticks on, the overflow fitting blocks, the drain is too slow — the consequences are swift and serious.

**What can go wrong without consistent maintenance:**

- **Timer failure** — pump stuck ON floods the table permanently; root rot sets in within 2–4 hours
- **Salt crust in LECA** — white calcium/mineral deposits accumulate in clay pebbles over weeks, binding nutrient ions and causing progressive lockout
- **Overflow fitting blockage** — debris or algae plugs the overflow; flood depth rises uncontrolled, submerging roots completely
- **Slow drain** — residual solution sits in table bottom after each cycle, creating anaerobic conditions and Pythium risk
- **Table liner micro-leaks** — tiny cracks in DIY pond-liner tables go unnoticed for weeks, losing solution slowly and skewing your reservoir level readings
- **Rain dilution** — open flood tables collect rainwater, crashing EC without you noticing

Ebb & Flow also has a major advantage: the entire media bed dries out and re-oxygenates between floods, which suppresses many diseases that plague continuously-wet systems. Maintaining proper drain-and-dry cycles is the single most important maintenance task.

Consistency is more important than intensity. Ten minutes every day is far more effective than three hours once a week.

## 2. Daily Tasks (10–20 minutes)

### Morning Routine (~10–15 min)

Check the system first thing, before the first scheduled flood of the day. This gives you a baseline reading before any flood cycle has run.

```
  DAILY MORNING CHECKLIST — EBB & FLOW:

  FLOOD CYCLE VERIFICATION
  [ ] Listen — is the pump audible? (reach into reservoir to feel vibration)
  [ ] Check timer display — does it show correct current time?
  [ ] Confirm last flood completed a full DRAIN: look at table surface —
      no standing water should remain from overnight flood
  [ ] If water is still standing in table (>5mm deep) → IMMEDIATE ACTION:
      check drain fitting, check overflow, test pump off manually

  SYSTEM HEALTH
  [ ] Check reservoir water level — note if more than 10% low (top-up needed)
  [ ] Visually check bulkhead fittings, table edges, and liner for drips or puddles
  [ ] Check table is level — no solution pooling in one corner
  [ ] Inspect around table base for evidence of slow leak (damp ground, staining)

  PLANT HEALTH
  [ ] Walk each table — look for wilting, yellowing, or collapsed plants
  [ ] Check undersides of 3–4 leaves per table for pests (aphids, mites, whitefly)
  [ ] Check media surface in each table — is LECA surface drying between floods?
      (surface LECA should be dry or barely moist 2+ hours after last flood)
  [ ] White crust visible on LECA surface? Note for weekly salt check.
  [ ] Note any unusual spots, mould, or webbing

  ZONE B — MICROGREENS
  [ ] Are trays in blackout phase? Check for any mould (white or green fuzzy growth)
  [ ] Are trays in light phase? Check moisture — mist if surface is dry
  [ ] Any trays ready to harvest? (cotyledons open, 5–8 cm tall)

  ZONE C — GROW BAGS
  [ ] Check media surface moisture — water if top 2–3 cm are dry
  [ ] Check for any pest damage on root veg leaves

  LOG
  [ ] Note date, flood status, and any observations in your logbook (see Section 7)
```

### Evening Top-Up (~5–10 min, as needed)

On hot days, combined plant transpiration and evaporation from the open table surface (E&F tables are more exposed than NFT channels) can drop the reservoir 5–10 L per day.

> **Realistic daily total:** In peak summer with all 3 zones active, expect 20–30 minutes total daily (morning check + evening top-up + any adjustments). In cool weather or with fewer plants, 10–15 minutes is typical. The times listed here are per-task minimums — add extra time when you spot issues.

```
  TOP-UP PROTOCOL (E&F specific):

  1. Check reservoir level — use fill marks on side of reservoir
  2. If level has dropped by >5 L, top-up is needed
  3. Prepare top-up water:
     a. Fill watering can/bucket with source water
     b. Adjust pH to 5.8–6.0 (water only — no nutrients)
     c. Do NOT add nutrients to top-up water (raises EC unpredictably)
  4. Pour into reservoir — confirm pump is still fully submerged
  5. Re-test EC and pH after top-up (dilution effect)
  6. If EC has dropped more than 0.3 mS/cm below target after top-up,
     a small nutrient addition may be needed

  NOTE ON E&F EVAPORATION:
  Open flood tables evaporate faster than enclosed NFT channels, especially
  on windy days. In summer, expect 20–30% more daily water loss than an
  equivalent NFT system. See Guide 10 for wind management options.

  WHICH WATER TO USE:
  - RO or rainwater (best): zero mineral addition per top-up
  - Soft tap water (EC <0.3): fine — minimal accumulation
  - Hard tap water (EC >0.5): mineral accumulation accelerates salt crust
    formation in LECA. Full reservoir changes every 7 days mandatory.
  See Guide 03 for water source analysis.
```

## 3. Twice-Weekly Tasks (15–20 minutes)

### EC and pH Measurement

Accurate EC and pH measurement is critical at least every 2–3 days. In an E&F system, the between-flood dry period concentrates any residual solution in the LECA by evaporation, which can push reservoir EC up faster than in continuous systems.

```
  EC AND pH TESTING PROTOCOL:

  Equipment:
  - Calibrated EC meter
  - Calibrated pH meter
  - Small cup or sampling container
  - Logbook

  Steps:
  1. Take sample from RESERVOIR (not from the drain return — this is
     already diluted by plant uptake and interaction with media)
  2. Take sample in the morning BEFORE the first flood of the day
     (reflects overnight concentration changes)
  3. Measure EC first — EC meters are robust; pH meters need care
  4. Record EC reading
  5. Rinse EC meter probe with clean water
  6. Measure pH
  7. Record pH reading
  8. Compare to targets for your current crop mix:
     - Lettuce/leafy greens: EC 1.0–1.6 mS/cm, pH 5.8–6.2
     - Herbs: EC 1.0–1.6 mS/cm, pH 5.8–6.2
     - Tomatoes/peppers: EC 2.0–3.5 mS/cm, pH 5.8–6.3
     - Strawberries: EC 1.2–1.8 mS/cm, pH 5.8–6.2
  9. Adjust as needed (see below)
  10. Rinse and dry meters; store pH meter in KCl storage solution

  RECORDING FORMAT (see Section 7 — logbook template):
  Date | EC | pH | Reservoir level | Flood cycles/day | Notes
```

### Actions Based on Readings

```
  pH ACTIONS:
  pH < 5.5: Add pH Up (potassium hydroxide) — 1 ml at a time, stir, retest
  pH 5.5–6.5: No action needed
  pH > 6.5: Add pH Down (phosphoric acid) — 1 ml at a time, stir, retest

  EC ACTIONS:
  EC < target: Add small amount of nutrients (mix stock concentrate and add)
               OR if more than 0.5 below target, do a partial reservoir change
  EC at target: No action needed
  EC > target by 0.2–0.4: Top up with plain pH-adjusted water (no nutrients)
  EC > target by 0.5+: Partial drain (remove 20–30% of solution) and replace
                        with pH-adjusted plain water to dilute

  E&F SPECIFIC NOTE — EC SPIKES:
  If EC jumps >0.3 mS/cm between readings in hot/dry weather, this is
  likely evaporation from the open table surface concentrating the residual
  solution in LECA. Increase top-up frequency before adjusting EC down.
```

## 4. Weekly Tasks (30–60 minutes)

### Full EC/pH Assessment and Adjustment

Same as twice-weekly measurement but with a more thorough check: inspect solution colour (clear = good; green-brown = algae or Pythium), check solution smell (fresh = good; musty/sour = root rot developing), and look at the table surface LECA for salt crust extent.

### Flood Cycle Verification

This is a uniquely important E&F maintenance task. Manually observe one complete flood cycle from start to finish.

```
  WEEKLY FLOOD CYCLE AUDIT (observe one full cycle):

  Step 1 — Pre-flood baseline:
  □ Note reservoir level before flood
  □ Confirm table surface is dry (LECA visibly dry or barely damp)
  □ Note time

  Step 2 — Flood rising:
  □ Once pump activates, watch solution rise in table
  □ Time to reach overflow fitting: typically 5–10 min
  □ Solution should reach overflow height and STOP rising
  □ If still rising above overflow: overflow fitting is BLOCKED → clear it
  □ Maximum flood depth: solution at overflow level, typically 2–5 cm above
    media surface (or at base of net pots for deep flood)

  Step 3 — Flood plateau:
  □ During flood, solution should remain steady at overflow height
  □ Listen — pump should maintain a steady hum (not laboured)

  Step 4 — Drain phase:
  □ When pump stops, time the drain
  □ Table should drain completely to dry within 20–30 min
  □ If still wet after 30 min: check drain fitting, check for debris blockage
  □ If still wet after 60 min: CRITICAL — see Guide 09, Section C2

  Step 5 — Post-drain:
  □ Confirm reservoir level is back to pre-flood level (±2 L for evaporation)
  □ Confirm no puddles under table (liner integrity check)
  □ Log: flood start time, drain-complete time, any issues
```

### Root Zone Inspection

```
  WEEKLY ROOT INSPECTION:

  Method 1 — Net pot lift:
  On each table, select 2–3 net pots and gently lift them out.
  In E&F, roots extend down through the LECA and pool at the table base.
  Inspect root mass emerging below the net pot:
  - White, dense, fine root hairs reaching downward: Excellent
  - Pale tan, thinner root mass: OK — ensure flood depth is adequate
  - Brown root tips: Early stress — check flood frequency and depth
  - Brown, slimy, foul smell: Active Pythium — see Guide 07 treatment
  Replace net pots immediately after inspection.

  Method 2 — Drain area peek:
  After a drain cycle, look through the drain fitting port into the
  table basin. You should see dry or barely damp LECA and white root
  threads. Any black slime or matted brown roots = root rot issue.
```

### Salt Crust and Media Check

Salt buildup in LECA is a defining maintenance issue for Ebb & Flow — it does not occur in the same way in NFT because solution flows through continuously. In E&F, every flood/drain cycle deposits a tiny residue of mineral salts. Over weeks, white crust forms on LECA surfaces.

```
  WEEKLY SALT CRUST CHECK:

  [ ] Inspect LECA surface in each table — white or grey powdery crust?
  [ ] Check around the sides and corners of the table — salt concentrates here
  [ ] Check net pot rims — visible white mineral ring?
  [ ] If minor crust (fine dusting only): normal — monitor
  [ ] If heavy crust (thick white layer, visible buildup):
      → Schedule media flush within 3 days (see Section 5)
  [ ] Check EC again — if EC is also rising, heavy crust is salt buildup
      from hard water rather than nutrient lockout from over-concentration

  INTERPRETING SALT CRUST COLOUR:
  White/grey: Calcium, magnesium, or carbonate deposits — water hardness
  Orange-brown tinge: Iron precipitation — check pH (keep below 6.5)
  Yellow: Sulfur or iron compounds — check nutrient formula ratio
```

### Algae and Biofilm Check

```
  WEEKLY ALGAE CHECK:

  [ ] Inspect table corners and walls below waterline (where algae grows first)
  [ ] Check overflow fitting tube interior — shine a torch through
  [ ] Check reservoir walls through the access lid
  [ ] Check fill/return inlet tube where it enters the table

  GREEN SLIME/ALGAE: Identify and eliminate the light source.
  Tables open to sky are more algae-prone than enclosed NFT channels.
  Shade cloth reduces algae growth as a secondary benefit.
  If algae established in table: remove plants, flush with 1% H₂O₂,
  rinse thoroughly with plain water. Replace plants.

  BIOFILM (grey/clear slimy coating on table surfaces): Normal at low levels.
  Excessive biofilm: indicator that reservoir change is overdue.
```

### Overflow Fitting Inspection

The overflow fitting is unique to E&F and is the single most critical fitting to keep clear. A blocked overflow prevents draining and causes permanent flooding.

```
  OVERFLOW FITTING WEEKLY CHECK:

  [ ] Remove overflow standpipe (the vertical tube in the overflow fitting)
      and inspect interior for debris, root threads, or algae growth
  [ ] Clear any material found — run a thin brush or pipe cleaner through it
  [ ] Confirm the overflow basket/screen (if fitted) is not clogged
  [ ] Re-insert standpipe and confirm it seats correctly
  [ ] During the next flood, observe overflow draining correctly when
      solution reaches the standpipe top
```

### Harvest and Succession Planting

```
  WEEKLY HARVEST AND REPLANT:

  [ ] Harvest ripe/ready crops:
      - Lettuce: pull heads or cut outer leaves
      - Herbs: snip as needed (pinch basil, cut chives and parsley)
      - Tomatoes/strawberries: pick ripe fruit
      - Root veg: check if ready (pull a test radish or carrot)

  [ ] Identify vacant net pot sites (harvested full plants)

  [ ] Check germination tray — are new seedlings ready to transplant?
      Transplant ready when:
      - 2–3 true leaves visible
      - Roots emerging from base/sides of rockwool cube or coco plug

  [ ] Transplant new seedlings to vacant sites
  [ ] Sow new seeds if seedling pipeline is running low
  [ ] Check microgreens succession — sow a new tray if pipeline needs it
```

### Nutrient Stock Solution Top-Up

```
  If using concentrated stock solutions (Masterblend trio or similar):
  [ ] Check stock solution containers — are they getting low?
  [ ] Prepare fresh stock solutions as needed (label with date)
  [ ] Stock solution shelf life: dissolved solutions 1–2 weeks; dry salts indefinitely
```

## 5. Periodic Deep Tasks (2–3 hours per session)

### Full Reservoir Drain and Clean

Even with good water management, nutrient salts accumulate, organic matter builds up, and microbial populations shift. A full drain and clean resets the system.

**Frequency depends on reservoir size and water quality:**
- **100 L reservoir (this system):** Full change every **10–14 days** — our 100 L reservoir is larger than a typical NFT reservoir relative to plant count, giving more buffer. If using hard tap water, change every 7 days.
- **If EC and pH are stable and plants look healthy:** You can extend to 14 days maximum.
- **After any pest or disease event, or after any root rot discovery:** Change immediately regardless of schedule.

```
  FULL RESERVOIR CHANGE:

  1. Before starting: run one flood cycle so tables are fully drained
     and all solution is back in the reservoir

  2. Drain all nutrient solution from reservoir:
     - Use pump or siphon hose to a bucket/drain
     - This solution can be diluted and used to water garden or lawn

  3. Clean reservoir interior:
     a. Wipe interior walls with clean cloth to remove sediment and biofilm
     b. Mix 10% bleach solution (100 ml bleach per 900 ml water)
     c. Coat all interior surfaces — let sit 10 minutes
     d. Drain bleach solution
     e. TRIPLE RINSE with clean water — no residual bleach

  4. Clean pump:
     a. Remove pump from reservoir
     b. Disassemble and rinse filter sponge in clean water
        (NOT tap water — chlorine kills any beneficial microbes on filter)
     c. Run pump briefly in a bucket of clean water to flush impeller
     d. Inspect impeller for calcium deposits — scrub with an old toothbrush

  5. Flush fill/return inlet line:
     a. Pour several litres of clean water into the fill line to clear debris
     b. Check the line for algae or biofilm inside

  6. Inspect all fittings:
     a. Check bulkhead fittings, grommets, and overflow standpipe
     b. Tighten or re-tape any weeping joints

  7. Refill and re-mix nutrient solution:
     a. Fill with fresh source water
     b. Mix nutrients (Masterblend or GH Flora) per recipe
     c. Check and adjust EC to target
     d. Check and adjust pH to 5.8–6.0
     e. Restart pump — run one manual test flood before re-setting timer

  8. Log date, volume, EC, pH, and any observations
```

### Media Flush and Salt Clearance

Unlike NFT, E&F LECA accumulates salts with each flood/drain cycle. Monthly flushing is essential to clear these deposits and restore nutrient availability.

```
  MONTHLY MEDIA FLUSH (one table at a time):

  1. Run the table's last flood cycle of the day before starting
  2. When drain is complete, remove all net pots from the table
     (place in a tray of dilute nutrient solution so roots stay wet)
  3. Set pump timer to manual ON or run a series of 3–5 rapid flood cycles
     using plain pH-adjusted water (no nutrients):
     - Fill table to overflow
     - Allow to drain fully
     - Repeat 3–5× until runoff water is clear and EC reading of the
       runoff is close to zero (0.1–0.2 mS/cm)
  4. On the final cycle, let the table drain and then check the LECA:
     - White crust should be visibly reduced or gone
     - LECA should feel loose again (not clumped by salt)
  5. Refill reservoir with fresh nutrient solution (reservoir will have
     been diluted significantly by the flush water — refill from scratch)
  6. Replace net pots and resume normal flood schedule
  7. If heavy crust remains after flush: consider removing all LECA from
     that table, soaking in a 5% citric acid solution overnight, rinsing
     thoroughly, and replacing (citric acid dissolves calcium carbonate)

  MONTHLY FLUSH FREQUENCY GUIDE:
  Soft water (EC <0.2) + RO: Flush every 6–8 weeks
  Tap water (EC 0.3–0.5):    Flush every 4 weeks
  Hard tap water (EC >0.5):  Flush every 2–3 weeks
```

### Table Liner Inspection

For DIY timber tables lined with pond liner, the liner requires periodic close inspection.

```
  MONTHLY LINER INSPECTION:

  1. After a media flush (LECA is still in table), look carefully at:
     a. All four corners — liner tends to fold and trap debris here,
        and fold stress lines are the first places cracks develop
     b. Around bulkhead fitting flanges — check for weeping or
        staining on the liner that indicates seepage behind the flange
     c. Liner surface below LECA — run hand across visible areas
        looking for any soft, wet, or compromised spots

  2. Every season (spring start): remove ALL LECA, inspect liner completely

  3. Signs that liner needs replacing:
     - Visible cracks or pinholes
     - Staining patterns from leaks
     - Brittleness or chalky texture (UV degradation)
     - Persistent low reservoir levels despite no visible external leak

  4. Emergency liner repair (small holes):
     Use PVC pond liner repair tape or aquatic silicone.
     Dry the liner completely before applying. Allow 24h cure.
```

### Meter Calibration

```
  MONTHLY METER CALIBRATION:

  pH Meter:
  [ ] pH 4.0 buffer solution (check expiry)
  [ ] pH 7.0 buffer solution (check expiry)
  [ ] Two-point calibration (see guide/03 Section 7)
  [ ] Record calibration date

  EC Meter:
  [ ] 1.413 mS/cm calibration solution
  [ ] Single-point calibration per meter instructions
  [ ] Record calibration date

  After calibration: measure plain tap water.
  pH should read your tap water's known pH (check water report).
  EC should read your tap water's known EC.
  This confirms calibration is correct.
```

### Grow Bag Media Refresh (Zone C)

```
  MONTHLY ZONE C CHECK:

  [ ] Check bags for compaction — is media still loose and airy?
  [ ] If compacted: aerate by pressing fingers through the media gently
  [ ] Check for fungus gnat larvae (tiny white worms at media surface)
  [ ] Flush bags: water thoroughly until 20% runoff (removes salt buildup)
  [ ] Check drainage — runoff should flow freely; if pooling, elevate
      bags or improve drainage under bag base
  [ ] Between crops: full media changeout per guide/05 recommendations
```

## 6. End-of-Season Tasks (3–6 hours total over 1–2 days)

### November Winterisation Checklist

```
  PLANT HARVEST AND REMOVAL:
  [ ] Harvest all remaining crops before first hard frost
  [ ] Remove all plants from E&F flood tables
  [ ] Harvest root veg from grow bags (don't leave in cold, wet media)
  [ ] Remove strawberry crowns — pot up in coco and bring to frost-free shelter
      (everbearing strawberries can be overwintered and replanted next spring)
  [ ] Cut back perennial herbs (mint, chives) to 5 cm and mulch

  E&F TABLE BREAKDOWN:
  [ ] Run final flood/drain cycle — table fully drained
  [ ] Remove all net pots from tables — sterilise and dry, store in sealed bag
  [ ] Remove all LECA from tables:
      → Rinse under running water until water runs clear
      → Soak in 10% bleach solution for 30 min
      → Triple rinse — no bleach residue
      → Spread on mesh or tarpaulin to dry in sun (1–2 days)
      → Store dry in sealed bags or bins
  [ ] Inspect pond liner in each table thoroughly now that it is exposed
  [ ] Clean table interior: brush out debris, wipe with dilute bleach solution,
      triple rinse, allow to dry
  [ ] For DIY timber tables: inspect timber frame for rot, swelling, or
      screw corrosion; treat exposed timber with water-based preservative

  FITTINGS AND PLUMBING:
  [ ] Remove overflow standpipes — clean, dry, label, store in a labelled bag
  [ ] Remove bulkhead fittings if possible (or cap with bung) to prevent
      freezing/cracking around the seals
  [ ] Check all rubber gaskets and O-rings — replace any that are cracked
  [ ] Drain fill/return hose completely — coil and store indoors
      (PVC hose becomes brittle if left outdoors over winter)
  [ ] Check thread tape on all joints — re-tape any that are starting to peel

  RESERVOIR:
  [ ] Drain reservoir completely
  [ ] Full reservoir sterilisation (10% bleach wash — triple rinse)
  [ ] Remove pump — clean fully, dry, store indoors
  [ ] Remove air stone (if used) — clean and store
  [ ] If reservoir is outdoors and cannot be moved: add a small amount of
      clean water and float a wooden board to absorb ice expansion pressure.
      DO NOT leave a sealed full reservoir exposed to freezing — it will crack.

  ZONE B (MICROGREENS):
  [ ] Clean all trays — bleach wash, triple rinse, dry and store
  [ ] Compost all used coco coir from trays (single use for microgreens)

  ZONE C (GROW BAGS):
  [ ] Compost all root veg media after harvest (used coco can go to garden compost)
  [ ] Clean grow bags — shake out, rinse, dry in sun
  [ ] Store bags flat in a dry place
  [ ] If reusing media next season: sterilise per guide/05 recommendations

  SHADE CLOTH AND ACCESSORIES:
  [ ] Clean shade cloth — brush off debris, rinse if dirty, dry before storage
  [ ] Store shade cloth rolled (not folded — fold creases crack the material)
  [ ] Store frost fleece similarly
  [ ] Store pH/EC meters in their cases with fresh storage solution in pH meter cap
  [ ] Store timer in a dry location — verify it holds its settings when unplugged
```

### Season-End Review

```
  REVIEW QUESTIONS (spend 30 min at end of season):

  1. Which crops performed best in the E&F tables? Which were disappointing?
  2. Was flood frequency correct for your conditions? Did roots ever look
     oxygen-stressed (from too many floods) or dry-stressed (too few)?
  3. How severe was the salt crust problem? What changes to media management
     or water source would reduce it next season?
  4. Did you have any timer failures or pump issues? What backup plan would help?
  5. Were there any liner or fitting leaks? What caused them?
  6. How was the rain/EC dilution problem? Did table covers help?
  7. What new crops do you want to try in the E&F tables next season?
  8. When did you run out of produce? Where are the gaps in succession planting?

  Document answers — they are invaluable for planning the next season.
```

## 8. Early Warning Signs Checklist

Catch problems before they become crises. Add these to your daily and weekly scan:

```
  EARLY WARNING SYSTEM — ACT IMMEDIATELY ON ANY OF THESE:

  FLOOD CYCLE SIGNALS:
  ⚠ Table still wet 30+ minutes after drain should have completed
    → drain fitting blocked, overflow too low, pump not off — CHECK TIMER AND DRAIN
  ⚠ Table not reaching expected flood depth → pump weak, or fill port partially blocked
  ⚠ Pump running but reservoir level dropping faster than normal
    → liner leak (solution is going into the ground, not draining back)
  ⚠ Pump sounds different (laboured, intermittent) → impeller clogged; inspect

  SOLUTION SIGNALS:
  ⚠ EC rising faster than normal despite no nutrients added → evaporation spike
    or rain is no longer diluting (check if cover is working the opposite way —
    catching runoff heat from roof)
  ⚠ EC dropping unexpectedly → rain dilution from open tables; check weather
  ⚠ Solution smells musty or foul → root rot developing — immediate reservoir check
  ⚠ Solution has turned green/brown → algae bloom or microbial growth
  ⚠ Heavy white crust on LECA across both tables → flush overdue, water too hard
  ⚠ Any dripping from bulkhead fittings → retape and reseal before it worsens

  PLANT SIGNALS:
  ⚠ Two or more plants wilting simultaneously → pump not flooding (timer fault)
    OR Pythium from over-flooding — check timer status FIRST
  ⚠ Wilting recovers after flood, recurs between floods → flood frequency too low
  ⚠ Roots consistently brown or slimy → over-flooding or poor drain — check drain time
  ⚠ Rapid yellowing spreading across multiple plants → pH out of range — test immediately
  ⚠ Tiny moving dots on leaf undersides → spider mites — treat immediately
  ⚠ Sticky residue on leaves → aphid honeydew — inspect closely
  ⚠ White powder on leaf surfaces → powdery mildew — remove affected leaves, treat
  ⚠ Small flies around the media → fungus gnats — deploy traps, treat media
  ⚠ Holes or skeletonised leaves → caterpillars or slugs — inspect at night
  ⚠ Tomato blossom drop → temperature stress or pollination failure — check conditions
```
